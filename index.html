The most honest perfumes are rarely literal. Novruz in Azerbaijan is not an inventory of sweets and spices. It is a feeling in the body: the loosening of the shoulders when the ice breaks, the relief of a house with its doors open.

GƏL BAHAR translates that feeling through olfactory illusion. It does not depict a dessert or a cut field of wheat. It offers cold green air, a rose at the moment of waking, wax warmed by a candle, and a fire sinking into resin. The nose supplies the rest, because the nose already knows.

THE BRIEF, ANSWERED

Lecheq Rose Oil is the emotional centre of the composition. Rose bases give it volume; the oil gives it truth. Everything else frames it. By pinning an intentional brake pad on the synthetics (lowering Hedione to 4.0% and Timbersilk to 7.0%), the traditionally thin middle gap is completely resolved. Supported by 2.0% Benzoin Siam, the formulation sits dense, deep, and beautifully substantive on the skin.

VISUAL LANDSCAPE

Chiaroscuro: Texture, Fire & Ice

The Vernal Shock

Young green wheat sprouts bursting through melting frost and glacial ice under sharp dawn light. Crisp, snapping, kinetic.

Key Notes: Cis-3-Hexenyl Acetate, Triplal, Yellow Mandarin

The Caucasus Heart

A magnificent bouquet of dark crimson Lecheq roses. Velvety, rich, and heavily texturized rather than airy or hollow.

Key Notes: Lecheq Rose Oil, Rose Givco 217, Wardia BM

The Purifying Embers

Bonfires at dusk, the leap over the flames, and sparks climbing into the dark. The fire sinks to a golden glow that stays warm long after the night has turned.

Key Notes: Whiskey Pentanol, CMP 147 Sweet Smoke, Labdanum Absolute, Benzoin Siam

The Xonça Table

A tray laid for the guest: şəkərbura, paxlava and qoğal, dried apricot, and a candle burning at the centre. Warm wax, honey and saffron light.

Key Notes: Beeswax Absolute, Osmanthus Absolute, Safraleine, Cardamom

The Samani Vow

Wheat grown in a dish for weeks and tied with a red ribbon. The smallest green promise that the world will begin again.

Key Notes: Petit Grain Heart, Limonene, Orange Flower Absolute, Indole

The Silk Thread

Hand-printed kəlağayı silk in crimson and gold, its buta motifs alive in the wind. The softness and movement the rose should leave on skin.

Key Notes: Timbersilk, Ambrettolide, Ambroxan, Hedione

THE STORY

From the Caspian wind to the last ember

It begins in the cold. A wind comes off the Caspian, carrying salt and the last of winter, and Baku pulls its coat tight. On the branches the ice is silver and faintly green at once.

Then comes a single drop, and another. On the windowsill, wheat has been growing in a dish for weeks. In the foothills of the Caucasus, the Lecheq roses stir before dawn, and the first of them opens.

Doors are opened. A tray is laid with sweets, apricots, a candle. In this country a guest is a blessing, and nobody leaves a house empty-handed.

At dusk the fires are lit. Children leap across the flames and the old year goes up in sparks. When the fire sinks, the rose does not fade. It deepens, warm with amber and a thread of smoke, and stays on the skin like the memory of a good night.

Gəl, Bahar.
